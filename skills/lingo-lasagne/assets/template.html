Mental model

The order a colorist works in. Each step depends on the one before.

  1. Normalize

    Turn the flat log footage into normal contrast, so every later step starts from a known base.

  2. Correct

    Fix exposure and white balance per shot, so the green cast disappears before any look is added.

  3. Match

    Make all shots of one scene look like one camera, so cuts do not jump.

  4. Look

    Add the creative style last, on top of matched shots, so it stays consistent.

Vocabulary ladder

From the first word a beginner meets to the word that marks a professional.

White balance

also: color temperature

The setting that makes white objects look white. A wrong white balance tints the whole picture.

Measured in Kelvin (K), plus a tint value on the green–magenta axis.

"Pull the tint toward magenta until the shirt in shot 3 is neutral."

Log footage

also: flat profile

Footage recorded with low contrast on purpose, to keep detail in shadows and highlights for the grade.

"Apply the camera's conversion LUT before you touch the contrast."

Vectorscope

A chart of the hue and saturation of every pixel. It shows a color cast your eyes have already got used to.

"Check the vectorscope: the skin tones must sit on the skin-tone line."

Classic confusions

The mix-ups that would make your next prompt wrong.

Correction is not a look

A film-look LUT on uncorrected shots bakes the green cast in. You recognise it when every shot keeps a different tint under the same look.

Your eyes are not a meter

After ten minutes the eye accepts a color cast as neutral. You recognise it when the scopes and your impression disagree.
